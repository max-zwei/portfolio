# Core

## What is the main message the infographic should communicate? And what are 3–5 key points or data insights to include? And who is the target audience?
- How have the Top 5 activities after sleep, work and eating changed over the four decades?
- What were the biggest shifts of activities?
- How have specific activites related to social life and media changed over the four decades?
- Which activities have a similar pairing of decline/incline to spark assumptions of how they are related?

## What is the target audience? What expectations and requirements do they come with?
Students at CODE University of Applied Sciences. The project focuses on ages 15–29 to align with the available datasets.

**Mindset:**
“Why should I bother about your information?”
“A paper poster is very boring and not exciting.”
This student needs a clear reason to stop and look. They are selective about where they spend their attention and may initially dismiss a paper poster as uninteresting. Their willingness to engage depends on how quickly the content establishes relevance to their own life.

**Motivation**
Discover something surprising about how people their age spend their time, and compare it with their own social life and media habits.

**Barriers**
- The topic feels abstract or disconnected from everyday experience.
- Dense text or complicated charts demand effort before offering a clear takeaway.
- The poster’s appearance gives little reason to expect something interesting.

**Needs**
- An immediate answer to “What does this have to do with me?”
- A clear main message that makes sense during a quick glance.
- Familiar visual references that invite attention.

**Design implications**
Establish a clear reading order and use concise copy, legible comparisons, and familiar cultural references. Ensure the visual hook supports the data story and that readers can choose how deeply to explore it.

**Desired response**
“That relates to my life. I understand what changed, and I want to look more closely.”

**Data Story**
From 1990s to 2020s the media technology changed drastically, from the introduction of the internet and personal computers , all the way to the fight over our attention span by large corporates with the help of always-available and quick-to-reach smartphones combined with fast paced media formats like short-form videos plus intentional addiciting design patterns like the endless scroll and personalised algorithms.

**Food for though**
But how did that affect the broad youth in Germany? Did it lead to a general new time share of how we go through our day? Or were only parts of the youth society affected by it?

**Visual Story**
To give an idea of the world between 1990s and 2020s, the pop culture is shown by famous memes as short and quickly graspable medium.
