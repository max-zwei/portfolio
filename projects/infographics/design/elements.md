# Numbers
_the peak of its activity code_

## 1990s
**ha_641 - Zu Besuch sein/Besuch empfangen**
mean: 39min per day
Visiting others or receiving guests peaked at an average of 39 minutes per day in the 1990s. People showed up at your door and expected a conversation. In your home. The doorbell was the notification, and ignoring it required hiding behind the sofa.

## 2000s
**ha_611 - Kontakte/ Gespräche mit Haushaltsmitgliedern (in Person)**
mean: 39min per day
The birth of Reality TV! Looks like it was inspired by real life... In the 2000s, in-person conversations with household members peaked at an averaged 38 minutes per day. An unpaid cast, a shared address, and a season-long investigation into who ate the leftovers. 

## 2010s
**ha_84 - Computer (PC, Laptop/Notebook, Netbook, Tablet-Computer) und  Smartphone**
mean: 33min per day
I’m slightly obsessed with the category name. "Computer and smartphone use" averaged 33 minutes per day in the 2010s. “Use” sounds so purposeful. So functioning and effective. And yet here we are, trying to rearrange a picture in Microsoft Word without smashing the screen.

# Trends
_trends from 1990s to 2020s_

## ha_110 Schlafen
+ 32 minutes
Somewhere between the clock radio and the phone alarm, sleep picked up another half hour. Did we get better at snoozing? Or what happened here? Or does our high-performance lifestyle just require more sleep?

## ha_627 Ausgehen (z.B. Cafés, Kneipen, Discos, Gaststätten)
- 12 minutes
Going out to cafés, pubs, clubs and restaurants decreased by 12 minutes per day on average from the 1990s to the 2020s. The disco ball has requested a meeting about its reduced role. It still turns. Apparently, that could have been an email.

## ha_961 Wegezeiten Soziale Kontakte
- 15 minutes
Travel for social contact fell by 15 minutes per day on average from the 1990s to the 2020s. Have we got too lazy to visit a friend and settled for sending a quick snap from the toilet? The chart cannot say. Please keep the camera above shoulder level.

## ha_45 Bauen renovieren, instand halten/reparieren, handwerkliche Tätigkeiten (Eigenleistungen)
- 11 minutes
DIY, renovation and repairs fell by 11 minutes per day on average from the 1990s to the 2020s. Why get your hands dirty when Etsy has a checkout button? “I made this” and “I found this” look suspiciously similar once the picture hits the feed.


# Comparisons
_comparisons of trends from 1990s to 2020s_

## ha_716 Gymnastik/Fitness/Gesundheitssport vs ha_811 Zeitung lesen
More time exercising, less time reading the newspaper. Perhaps we’ve stopped following world events and started physically preparing for them. Difficult to say. But apparently the appropriate response to the morning headlines is no longer a second coffee. It’s core strength.

## ha_921 Wegezeiten zur Haupterwerbstätigkeit vs ha_412 Backen
7
More time commuting, less time baking. So much for the home-office sourdough fantasy. These trends suggest the starter has been abandoned so its owner can sit in traffic. Both activities involve waiting for something to rise. Unfortunately, this one is your blood pressure.

## ha_120 Essen und Trinken vs ha_611 Kontakte/ Gespräche mit Haushaltsmitgliedern (in Person)
16
More time eating and drinking, less time talking to the people we live with. Perhaps “don’t talk with your mouth full” was the most effective communication policy ever introduced. Another helping? Absolutely. A conversation about how we’re doing? Sorry. Still chewing.

## ha_626 Zirkus/Vergnügungsparks/Kirmes/Volksfest/Messen besuchen, Ausflüge vs ha_819 Lesen
8
More time on outings and attractions, less time reading. Perhaps we still enjoy a good story. We just want it to last ninety seconds and turn us upside down. The emotional arc is similar. Anticipation, conflict, a brief confrontation with mortality. Then someone tries to sell you a photograph of your lowest point.
